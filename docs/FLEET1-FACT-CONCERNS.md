# Fleet 1 — factConcerns (editorial review)

## omega-and-the-olympic-clock
The 1948 slit photo-finish camera was developed by the British Race Finish Recording Company, not Omega; the body attributes it to 'London' generically rather than to Omega. 'A few millimeters of moving swimmer / finer than pool construction tolerances' paraphrases the widely reported rationale for the post-1972 rule change without citing exact figures. Omega's '31 occasions' count is attributed to the company since edition counts vary by whether Youth and Paralympic Games are included. '55.2 to match the winner's' reflects the official results table (both credited 55.2) per ISHOF and Wikipedia; the exact administrative mechanism of raising Larson's time is reported secondhand. 1932 'first' phrasing follows Omega's own chronicle ('first watchmaker to time an entire Olympic Games').

## what-a-chronometer-actually-is
Excellence Chronometer specs and dates verified across two independent trade outlets (Monochrome, Time and Watches) but still not against a primary COSC document — cosc.swiss did not surface one; re-check before publish. Certificate-volume and brand-ranking figures rest on Wikipedia, whose numbers are undated and slightly self-inconsistent (total 'over a million' vs Rolex alone 'over a million'); body deliberately says only 'more than a million' and 'a few percent of Swiss production'. COSC's own page describes the full protocol as 12–20 days with intensive measurement on days 2–15; 'fifteen days' is the ISO 3159 test duration both COSC's FAQ and Wikipedia use, so the body keeps it. Closing arithmetic (6 s/day ≈ 0.25 s/hour; <42 s/week) is derivation, not sourced.

## the-escapement-the-part-that-lets-go
The 'five impacts per tick' figure is single-sourced to Horopedia; the Watch-O-Scope timing manual refers to 'the three sounds made by each and every tick' (machine-resolved pulses), so the count depends on acoustic resolution — body hedges with 'by the horologist's count'. Mudge's invention date varies by source (Wikipedia ~1754, Royal Collection Trust 'by 1755', Seiko Museum ~1756); body and tldr say 'mid-1750s'. Draw angle ranges differ (Wikipedia 11-15 degrees, Horopedia 13-16); body hedges to 'a dozen-odd degrees'. Escape-wheel revolution time is derived arithmetic: 15 teeth x 2 beats = 30 beats per turn at 8 beats/s = 3.75 s, stated as 'roughly every four seconds'. Cut from the draft as unverifiable: the '40-60 degrees of contact' figure (not present in the cited retailer blog), the 'out past 270 degrees' amplitude figure, and the 'degree or two' lock depth. Shellac-set pallet stones and micron-level adjustment are standard bench practice described across service literature; no single URL. The Quill & Pad frequency source returned 503 at check time and was replaced with Watch-O-Scope and Caliber Corner.

## when-to-service-a-mechanical-watch
Rolex ~10-year quote and Omega 5–8-year interval plus annual water-resistance check verified directly on brand pages. Independent-service price ranges come from secondary repair-industry guides (weknowwatches, valueyourwatch), not brand lists; stated as hedged ranges. 'Weeks, sometimes months' turnaround is common industry experience stated as expectation, not sourced to a specific page. 'Several days of observation' after service is standard workshop practice, lightly hedged. The four-to-seven-year convention is an aggregate of brand and watchmaker guidance rather than a single citable figure.

## your-first-automatic-what-matters
Service-interval guidance now spans the verified range (3-5 years worn hard, up to ~10 at rest, 5 max for swimmers per Crown & Caliber's industry panel) rather than a single 5-7 figure; still varies by brand and no single standard exists. 'Tens of seconds a day' drift on the cheapest calibers tracks the NH35's published -20/+40 s/day tolerance but no spec number is stated in the body. Magnetism effect softened to 'throw the rate hard in either direction' because the verified Seiko source says gain OR lose, not specifically fast; the 'minutes a day' figure was cut as unverified. FAQ claim that racket sports and barbell impacts stress movements is standard enthusiast/watchmaker guidance, hedged, not tied to a single source. Lug-to-lug wrist-flat rule is enthusiast convention, not a formal standard.

## the-waterproof-watch-and-the-open-water
Re-verified 2026-07-01. Confirmed against two or more independent sources: patent 114948 (30 Oct 1925, La Chaux-de-Fonds); the no-clutch flaw and the 18 Oct 1926 Rolex registration with clutch fix (vintagewatchstraps + Wind Vintage), so the earlier heavy hedge was dropped; Borgel 1891 Geneva patent (body tightened from 'from the 1890s' to the patent date); Depollier NBS testing and Nov 1918 Signal Corps order of 10,000 cases (the 10,000 figure is now multi-source but the body keeps 'in quantity'); Tavannes Submarine (1916, screw back and bezel); Gleitze vindication swim 21 Oct 1927, watch on a ribbon, 10h24m immersion per her own statement; Daily Mail front-page ad 24 Nov 1927 (Rolex confirms front page and full page, secondary sources supply the date; body keeps 'the following month'); fluted bezel's original tool-grip function (Rolex's own page). Still soft: (1) the Wilsdorf 'like its namesake' quote is ubiquitous in secondary sources but no primary 1926 document was traced — lead-in now says 'a line still quoted' rather than attributing it to current company copy; (2) the exact year of the NBS test is not pinned in the sources checked, so the body ties 1918 to the Signal Corps order instead; (3) 'small gold Oyster' remains the hedged form since the 33mm case size is single-source; (4) whether Rolex acquired the patent rights in 1925 or July 1926 varies by source, so the body gives no acquisition date; (5) 'first waterproof watch' remains treated as marketing, stated as contested in the body; (6) journal.hautehorlogerie.org returned 503 during this pass — kept as a background source only, no claim rests solely on it; vintagewatchstraps and perezcope block automated fetchers (403) but their content was corroborated via search excerpts.
