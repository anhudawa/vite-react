The list price is USD 3,700, checked this week on Breitling's own page, and the watch
weighs 64.5 grams on its rubber strap. Held together, those two figures are the whole
argument: mechanical-chronograph money for a quartz chronograph in a composite case,
spent on the theory that a racing wrist needs lightness and accuracy more than it needs
romance. One thing said plainly at the top: no one at this desk has worn it yet. This
review is written from the maker's published sheet, from the reviewers who have had the
watch on a scale, and from the Ironman seasons it has been raced through since Breitling
became the series' official luxury watch partner in 2021. Where a review would usually
tell you how the pushers feel at hour four, this one quotes someone who knows or stays
quiet. [The guide to this watch](/guides/the-breitling-endurance-pro) covers what it is
and who wears it; this piece is only the verdict.

## Accuracy in use

The movement is the reason the watch exists. Caliber 82 is a thermocompensated
SuperQuartz, chronometer-certified, and the figure that follows it through the coverage
is about ten seconds a year. [A mechanical chronometer](/guides/what-a-chronometer-actually-is)
is allowed to drift that far in two days and keep its certificate; ordinary quartz does
it in a month. Thermocompensation earns the difference — a sensor reads the temperature
and corrects the crystal's rate, so the watch holds its figure in cold open water at
seven in the morning and on hot tarmac at noon. For a chronograph whose dial reads to a
tenth of a second, that is the spec and the promise agreeing with each other. Most
watches at this price drift more in a week than this one is rated to drift in a year.

## Weight, and the water

Breitlight, the proprietary composite, is quoted by Breitling at 3.3 times lighter than
titanium and 5.8 times lighter than steel. On Fratello's scales the finished 44 mm watch
comes to 64.5 grams on the rubber; Watch Advice's hands-on agrees at roughly 65. That is
running-watch mass in a 44 mm by 12.5 mm case, and mass is the spec that changes
ownership: a watch you stop noticing is a watch that stays on for the interval session,
which is the entire premise of paying luxury money for a tool.

Water is the sheet's one soft line. The rating is 10 bar — 100 metres — which
[clears swimming and open water](/guides/water-resistance-for-swimmers) with room to
spare, and the case material is indifferent to sweat and salt in a way plated metal
never is. But the crown is not screw-locked; it seals on two gaskets. Breitling
evidently trusts the arrangement, and the rating stands behind it. Watch Advice still
called 100 m light for the watch's positioning, and on a sports case at this price a
screw-down crown would have been a cheap reassurance. A real limitation, then, if a
small one.

## Straps, batteries, ownership

The lugs are 22 mm and unfussy about it: the Diver Pro rubber strap ships in five
colours, Breitling sells a woven ECONYL alternative, and anything 22 mm in a drawer goes
straight on. The buckle is Breitlight too, so the strap end adds almost nothing back.
Upkeep is where quartz quietly wins the ledger. There is no escapement to regulate and
no [periodic overhaul to budget](/features/what-it-costs-to-keep-time); there is a
battery, and its life is the one figure the coverage disagrees on — three to four years
by most accounts, about two by one, no number from Breitling. Call it a counter visit
every few years and the honest answer is that ownership is undramatic, which for this
watch is a feature.

## The price, read both ways

USD 3,700 is the list, before tax, as of 7 July 2026. Read one way it is absurd: a GPS
watch costing a fraction of that will pace, navigate and record, while this times an
effort to a tenth and tells you nothing else. Read the other way, it undercuts the steel
mechanical chronographs it shares a display case with and beats them on every criterion
above except the romance of a balance wheel. The buyer it suits is not cross-shopping
function. [The GPS watch already owns the data](/guides/gps-watch-vs-mechanical); this
is the one luxury object built to be worn while the data is being made.

## Verdict

Buy it if you are the athlete this site keeps writing about — training most days,
swimming some of them, owning one good watch and intending to wear it through all of it.
At the price, nothing matches the combination of chronometer-grade timekeeping, a case
you forget, straps that swap in a minute and upkeep measured in batteries.

Skip it if what you want from USD 3,700 is a movement with a heartbeat; that is what
most of this site is for, and this watch will never be that. Skip it if you need a true
dive rating or expect the watch to record anything at all. And if 44 mm reads as a shelf
on [a lean wrist](/guides/sizing-a-watch-for-a-lean-wrist), the 38 exists at 53 grams
and gives up little beyond presence.

The tension is worth owning out loud: a mechanical-first magazine recommending quartz.
The Endurance Pro earns it precisely because it does not apologise. On race morning the
grails stay in the drawer, and this one gets to go.
