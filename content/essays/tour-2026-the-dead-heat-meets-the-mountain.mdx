For five days the 2026 Tour de France carried a tie at the top: Tadej Pogačar and
Jonas Vingegaard level on accumulated time, to the second, through everything the
road had offered since Barcelona. [The week-one dispatch](/dispatch/tour-2026-week-one-on-the-clock)
ended with the mountains that would settle it. They needed one afternoon. Pogačar
rode away from everyone on the Col du Tourmalet, won alone in Gavarnie-Gèdre by
2:38, and pulled on a yellow jersey that had started the day 7:53 up the road on
another man's shoulders.

## Four point one kilometres

The stage handed the race its first hors-catégorie climb: 186 kilometres from Pau
over the Aspin and the Tourmalet, the big summit crested 38.5 kilometres from the
line. UAE Team Emirates rode it like a team holding one card and certain of it.
Isaac del Toro accelerated out of what remained of the favourites' group high on
the Tourmalet with Pogačar on his wheel, nobody else able to follow, and 4.1
kilometres below the summit the world champion went on alone. He led over the top
by about thirty seconds from Vingegaard, collecting the Souvenir Jacques Goddet,
the prize for cresting the Tourmalet first — which, by one of cycling's odder
curses, has never yet gone to the man who went on to win that Tour.

A lone rider forty kilometres out is usually a loan the bunch recalls. This one
compounded. The gap grew on the descent and kept growing up the drag to
Gavarnie-Gèdre, 18.7 kilometres at 3.7 per cent, the kind of climb raced in the
big ring. Vingegaard spent it alone in between, too strong for the chasers and
nowhere near the leader, and held second by nineteen seconds from del Toro, who
led the chase home for third at 2:57 ahead of Remco Evenepoel and Paul Seixas.

The sum at the top of the race is brutally simple again. Dead heat, plus 2:38 on
the road, plus the four seconds between the winner's bonus and the runner-up's:
Pogačar leads Vingegaard by 2:42. Del Toro sits third at 3:27, Evenepoel fourth
at 3:30, Juan Ayuso fifth at 3:34. Stage 4's thirteen-minute advance to the
breakaway, the loan that put a Norwegian in yellow, was called in on a single
climb.

## Two days in yellow

Torstein Træen's race turned before his crash did. The Tourmalet dropped him
early, and the 7:53 he held over the favourites was mostly surrendered on the
ascent itself, long before Pogačar moved. Then, on the descent, he touched wheels
with his own teammate Anders Halland Johannessen in a corner and went down. He
was back on his feet quickly, passed a roadside concussion check, and rode to the
finish; first reports put the day's total loss at around twenty minutes. He had
been clear-eyed about this on Wednesday: "Tadej is Tadej, and I think if he goes
full gas on the Tourmalet, maybe I will be behind." Two days in yellow, honestly
come by, and the crash at the end of it read as bad luck piled on a verdict
already delivered.

The mountain claimed one rider outright: Cian Uijtdebroeks, dropped early,
climbed off on the Tourmalet and is out of the race.

## The bus fare

Further back, the day ran on the other deadline. Wednesday's winner Olav Kooij
and the rest of the fast men crossed the Aspin and the Tourmalet against the
elimination cut, a percentage of Pogačar's 4:32:07, and [the bus did that
arithmetic collectively, out of camera shot](/features/the-autobus). No
eliminations had been reported by Thursday evening, and the reward for the
survivors is immediate: Friday runs 175.1 flat kilometres from Hagetmau to
Bordeaux, one small rise all day, into a city that has been paying off sprinters
since 1903.

So the question week one kept open is closed. A dead heat holds only while the
road asks questions both riders answer identically, and the Tourmalet asked one
of its own. For the first time since Barcelona the top of this Tour is a gap
rather than a tie: 2:42, written in one afternoon, with two weeks left to decide
whether it was the opening argument or the whole case.
