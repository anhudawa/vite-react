A post went around. Lionel Messi, at the 2026 World Cup, wearing a "newly released
off-catalogue 'Jubilee Gold' Rolex Day-Date 40 with a green stone dial." MSRP $62,700.
"Easily a $350k–$400k+ piece." Seventy-nine likes and climbing.

This is exactly the kind of thing we exist to run through the mill. So we did. Here is
what survived.

## Who posted it

The account is "The Luxury Watch Guy" — an anonymous watch dealer out of Manhattan's
Diamond District, with a blue tick (the paid kind), a storefront, and a commercial
interest in exactly one thing: the luxury-watch market running hot. That doesn't make
him wrong. It makes him a lead, not a source.

## What's true

Most of it, as it turns out.

The watch is real: a Rolex Day-Date 40 in "Jubilee Gold," a new in-house alloy, with a
natural green aventurine stone dial and baguette-cut diamond markers — reference
228235JG, shown at Watches and Wonders 2026, and genuinely off-catalogue, the kind of
piece Rolex hands to a short list of its most loyal clients. The roughly $62,700 retail
figure checks out across the watch press.

And it is a current sighting, not a recycled old photo. The easy assumption, given the
World Cup backdrop, is that this is December 2022 all over again. It isn't. 2026 is
itself a World Cup year, and the watch did not exist in 2022. Messi wore it around the
match where he put three past Algeria and drew level with Miroslav Klose's all-time
World Cup scoring record. Robb Report logged the same sighting; the match is on the
record with FIFA. This one holds up.

## What doesn't

The number. "$350k–$400k+" is the dealer's estimate, not a fact. The grey market for
this reference is all "price on request" — no published transactions, no proof of a
quarter-million-dollar premium over retail. A big markup on an off-catalogue diamond
Day-Date is plausible. Plausible is not sourced. We would run the $62,700 retail figure
and stop there.

## The part everyone gets wrong

Here is the correction that matters, and that almost nobody makes: **Messi is not a
Rolex ambassador.** Rolex does not do celebrity watch ambassadors of this kind. Messi's
paid watch deal is with Jacob & Co. The Rolexes — this green Day-Date, the platinum one
he wore lifting the trophy in 2022, the John Mayer Daytona — he buys himself, or is
allocated as the sort of client every boutique competes for.

Which lands it, neatly, on the right side of the only line we really care about: this is
not a placement. Nobody paid Messi to wear it. He bought it. That is a different — and
frankly more interesting — fact than "sponsored athlete wears sponsor's watch," and it
is the one the viral post, and most of the coverage chasing it, never bothered to get
straight.

The watch is the easy part. Who paid for it is the whole story.
