Lionel Messi spent a night at the 2026 World Cup putting three past Algeria and drawing
level with Miroslav Klose as the tournament's all-time leading scorer. On his wrist while
he did it: one of the rarest Rolexes money can't quite buy.

It's a Rolex Day-Date 40 in "Jubilee Gold," a new in-house alloy, with a dial cut from a
single slab of green aventurine and ten baguette diamonds for markers — reference
228235JG, unveiled at Watches and Wonders 2026. "Off-catalogue," in Rolex's language,
means it isn't for sale in the ordinary way: it goes to a short list of the brand's most
valued clients, in tiny numbers, at a retail price around $62,700.

That $62,700 is the real number. You'll see the watch called a "$350,000–$400,000 piece"
online — that's a guess. The grey market for this reference is all "price on request"
and no public sales; a steep premium is plausible, but plausible isn't a price. The
sticker is $62,700. Everything above it is someone's hope.

## The part worth knowing

Here's the fact almost every write-up gets wrong: **Messi is not a Rolex ambassador.**
Rolex doesn't pay footballers to wear watches. Messi's paid watch deal is with Jacob &
Co. The Rolexes — this green Day-Date, the platinum one he wore lifting the trophy in
2022, the John Mayer Daytona — he buys himself, as the kind of client every boutique
wants walking through the door.

<Pull>
  Nobody paid Messi to wear it. He bought it. That's the more interesting fact — and the
  one everyone skips.
</Pull>

Which puts it on the right side of the only line we care about. A sponsored athlete in a
sponsor's watch is an advertisement. A man quietly acquiring one of the hardest watches
in the world to get hold of — and wearing it to play the biggest match of his year — is
something else entirely. That's not a placement. That's a collector who happens to be the
greatest footballer alive.

The watch is extraordinary. That he paid for it himself is the story.
