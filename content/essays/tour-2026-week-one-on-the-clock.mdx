Four stages into the 2026 Tour de France, the two best stage racers of the era are
tied. Level on accumulated time, to the second, after thirteen hours of racing since
Barcelona. Tadej Pogačar took the yellow jersey on Monday only because the rulebook
keeps a tie-break for the days the clock declines to choose, and by Tuesday evening
neither he nor Jonas Vingegaard led the race at all. [The first dispatch read this
Tour from the wrist down](/dispatch/the-wrists-of-the-2026-tour). This one reads the
ledger the race actually keeps.

## Twelve, six, zero

The first seconds were bought in Barcelona on Saturday, by teams rather than men. The
opening team time trial, 19.6 kilometres ending on Montjuïc under a new format that
gave every rider his own time, went to Visma–Lease a Bike, with Vingegaard attacking
the final climb to make sure the race's first yellow jersey was his. UAE Team Emirates
finished 12 seconds slower. Opening balance: Vingegaard 12, Pogačar 0.

Sunday the race climbed Montjuïc again, three circuits this time, and the ledger took
its first bonus-second entry. Isaac del Toro won the uphill sprint, waved through by
Pogačar, who sat on his young teammate's wheel and settled for second place and the
six bonus seconds it pays. Twelve became six.

Monday brought the first summit finish, 195.9 kilometres from Granollers to Les
Angles, and Pogačar rode away from Vingegaard on the final ramps to win by two
seconds. Two gained on the road, plus the winner's ten bonus seconds against the six
Vingegaard collected for second: six seconds recovered, precisely. Twelve, six, zero.
Three stages, two countries, one Pyrenean summit, and the sum at the top of the
general classification was a dead heat.

A jersey still has to go to somebody, and it went to Pogačar on countback, the
tie-break the sport holds in reserve for a result the clock scores level. This site
spent a whole essay on [the eight seconds that settled the 1989
Tour](/features/the-1989-tour-eight-seconds), the closest in its history. On Monday
night the gap between the two favourites of this one was zero.

## Thirteen minutes, given away

On Tuesday the race did the opposite thing. It spent time deliberately, in bulk.
Thirty-four riders went clear on the road to Foix, ten survived the foothills, and
Mads Pedersen won the stage while the peloton, UAE having called off the chase, rolled
in 12 minutes and 59 seconds later. At the sharp end this counts as good business.
Time, on a day like this, is the cheapest thing a favourite can spend; energy is
dearer, and Paris is nineteen days away.

The man who collected was Torstein Træen of Uno-X Mobility, who began the stage 5:06
down on Pogačar, finished eighth in the break, and now benefits from the only
arithmetic that matters: 12:59 gained, minus 5:06 owed, leaves 7:53 in hand. He leads
the Tour by 28 seconds from Sean Quinn, a companion from the move, with Mathias Vacek
at 3:50 and then, at 7:53, the two men whose race this really is, fourth and fifth,
still level with each other to the second. Remco Evenepoel sits at 8:16, del Toro at
8:17, Juan Ayuso at 8:20. Paul Seixas, the teenager with the five-day-old Breitling
deal, rounds out the top ten at 8:41.

Read as effort, the standings look strange. Read as time, they are simple. Everything
above fourth place is a loan. The favourites let the jersey go because defending it
costs a team weeks of work it would rather bank, and 7:53 is the size of the advance
they judged safe. The mountains will set the interest.

## The other deadline

Wednesday runs 158.3 flat kilometres from Lannemezan to Pau, a sprinters' day at last,
and the fast men have earned it, having been made to climb to a Pyrenean ski station
inside the first three stages. Their July answers to a different number anyway: the
elimination cut, a percentage of a winning time that does not exist until the winner
posts it. When the high mountains arrive, [the bus will form and do that maths
together](/features/the-autobus). This week it was spared, and the clock did its work
at the front instead.

Four days, one dead heat, one countback, one thirteen-minute gift. The watches on the
start line are decoration, as promised. The clock at the finish has already decided
everything, including, once, deciding nothing at all.
