A club ten-mile time trial starts in a lay-by at seven on a Sunday, and nobody there is
dressed to impress a watch. There is a man with a numbered card safety-pinned to his back,
warming up against the cold by riding small circles in the road, and on his wrist, under the
cuff of a long-sleeve base layer, is a little black Casio with a resin strap. Not the good
watch. The good watch is at home in a drawer, where it cannot get rained on or knocked off a
bike rack. This is the one that came out to work.

You know the watch I mean even if you have never owned one. The F-91W. Black plastic, a small
grey screen, a face you have seen on a thousand wrists and never once looked at twice. It is
the watch you buy without ceremony and stop thinking about by the afternoon. And it is, by a
distance, the most worn watch I can think of that nobody has ever framed a story around.

So here is the story.

## The watch that goes when the good one stays

There is a particular honesty to the cheap beater, and it is not the honesty of [a sealed case](/features/the-waterproof-watch-and-the-open-water)
proving itself in cold water. It is humbler than that. The F-91W is honest because it asks for
nothing. It does not want to be admired. It does not want to be insured. It barely registers
on the wrist — light enough that you forget it is there, which on a time-trial morning is
exactly the point, because the last thing you want at threshold is to be aware of anything you
do not have to be.

The man in the lay-by chose it for reasons he could list in one breath. It runs for years on
a battery he will probably never bother to change, replacing the whole watch instead when it
finally stops. It does not care about the rain. If it cracks against the top tube on a clumsy
mount, the loss is pocket change and an afternoon's mild annoyance. And it has, buried in those
two side buttons, the only thing he actually needs from it for the next twenty-some minutes of
sustained pain: it can time him.

That is the quiet joke of the F-91W. It is famous for being cheap, and what it does for the
money is the oldest serious thing a watch can do.

## A stopwatch the size of nothing

Strip a ten-mile time trial down to [its instrument](/features/the-instrument-of-effort) and you do not need much. You need a clock
that starts when you do and reports an interval you can read at a glance, mid-effort, with your
heart somewhere up around its ceiling. A little plastic Casio with a stopwatch and a small alarm
does precisely this and refuses to do anything else, and the refusal is the feature.

I have come to think the cult around this watch is earned, not ironic. People reach for irony
because the thing is cheap and ubiquitous and looks like nothing, and they assume the affection
must be a joke at the watch's expense. It is not. The F-91W is loved the way a good cheap tool
is loved — the screwdriver that has been in the drawer for fifteen years, the kettle that has
never once failed to boil. It does the job it was shaped for, it does it for a span of years that
feels faintly absurd given what it costs, and it never once pretends to be more than it is. In a
hobby that can curdle into status very quickly, that is close to a moral position.

## Exactly enough, and nothing more

The collector's instinct is to want more watch. More complication, more finish, more provenance,
more weight on the wrist to remind you it is there. The F-91W is the argument from the other
direction, and it is the one our man in the lay-by is making without meaning to make it. He owns
a watch he loves. He left it at home. He is racing in something he could lose down a drain and
feel nothing, and it is keeping perfect time on a morning when perfect time is the entire job.

There is a freedom in that I do not think the good watch can give you. A nice mechanical piece
asks to be looked after, and looking after it is part of the pleasure, but it is also a small
tax you pay every time you wear it somewhere that might hurt it. The cheap quartz beater charges
no tax. You stop curating. You strap it on and go and ride your eyeballs out and never once think
about the watch, which is the highest compliment an instrument can be paid, because it means it
disappeared into the work.

I should be clear about where I stand, because this site has a centre and the centre is
mechanical. [The escapement](/features/the-escapement-the-part-that-lets-go), the heritage, the watches earned in the field over a century — that
is the love, and it is not in any doubt here. But a brand built on honesty about endurance owes
the F-91W a paragraph at least, because it is the most honest object in the whole drawer. It
makes no claim it cannot keep. It costs almost nothing and asks for less. And when the result
sheet goes up on the village hall door an hour later, the time next to that man's name was kept,
faithfully, by the cheapest watch on the start line.

That is not a watch to roll your eyes at. It is a watch to take your hat off to, quietly, on a
cold Sunday morning, and then go and get a coffee.
